SLIDE 3 · §3 TECHNIQUES

B · Bhone Pyae Hein · The makingAbout 1:30Banner: band along the bottom

How the models learn

On screen

How the models learn
  • Supervised learning on labelled data: 7,637 sea photos (Open Images, Aquarium Combined) and 13,825 sound clips (FSD50K, Freesound)
  • Transfer learning: fine-tuned pretrained CNNs, YOLO26 Nano for detection and EfficientAT mn10 (AudioSet) for sound
  • Train / validation / test split; the test set stays frozen: 767 photos and 4,216 clips never seen in training
  • Sound is multi-label: spectrograms of 1 s slices every 0.5 s, several sounds at once

Banner shows

The lab's sample photos travel, one at a time, through COLLECT → TRAIN → TEST → SHIP. At TEST each photo gets the box and score our real model gave it (for example "dolphin 66%"). (SAMPLE.)

Points to cover

  • Supervised learning: every training photo has boxes drawn around each creature (22,718 labelled creatures across 7,637 photos); every sound clip is labelled with which sounds it contains.
  • Transfer learning: we didn't train from zero. YOLO26 Nano (2.5 million parameters) already knew general shapes; EfficientAT mn10 was pretrained on AudioSet. We fine-tuned them on our data.
  • Fair split: sea photos 5,312 train · 1,558 validation · 767 test. Sound clips 8,617 train · 992 validation · 4,216 test. The test set is never used for training or tuning.
  • Multi-label sound: audio is turned into a spectrogram (a picture of sound). The model looks at 1-second slices every half second, and one slice can contain several sounds at once.

SLIDE 4 · §4 TECHNIQUES

B · Bhone Pyae Hein · The makingAbout 1:30Banner: tall strip on the right

How they predict, and how we measure

On screen

How they predict, and how we measure
  • Object detection: boxes with confidence; IoU and non-max suppression remove duplicates
  • Thresholds: 45% for creatures; per-sound thresholds tuned on validation, never on test
  • Metrics: mAP@50, mean average precision and F1, plus a list of how each model fails
  • On-device inference: exported to ONNX and run by ONNX Runtime Web in the browser

Banner shows

A phone: the ONNX model streams in from above as particles, a photo inside gets its real box, a shield pulses, "PHOTO STAYS HERE". (SAMPLE.)

Points to cover

  • Object detection: the model outputs boxes with a confidence. Overlapping boxes for the same animal are measured with IoU (how much two boxes overlap) and non-max suppression keeps only the best one.
  • Thresholds: a creature is shown only if the model is at least 45% sure. Each of the 17 sounds has its own threshold, tuned on the validation set, so the test score stays honest.
  • Metrics: mAP@50 means a box counts as correct when it overlaps the true box by at least 50%. For sound we also report F1: 0.59 second-by-second on 97 test mixes.
  • On-device: both models are exported to ONNX and run with ONNX Runtime Web inside the browser. Nothing is uploaded.

Banner shows

The real model output for the sample "Two sounds at once": a playhead runs down the clip and bars grow for speech, engine / traffic and clapping, with their real confidences (68%, 75%, 99%…).

Points to cover

  • It finds 17 everyday sounds (dog bark, siren, glass breaking, knocking…) and places them on a timeline. Three ways in: record, upload, or listen live (live mode stops by itself after 2 minutes).
  • Score: 0.79 mean average precision on 4,216 test clips.
  • A design choice: the bigger mn10 model scores 0.79 at 20.5 MB; the smaller mn04 scores 0.76 at 6.5 MB. We chose accuracy.
  • Honest weak spots: doorbell is the weakest sound (0.46); footsteps and car horn are also weak; timing is rough: it finds 49% of sounds within 0.5 s of when they start.

RAG assistants

On screen

IN PROGRESS
RAG assistants
  • Answers from its own documents: retrieve the right passages, then a small language model writes the reply
  • Runs in the browser: Qwen2.5-1.5B and snowflake-arctic-embed-s on WebLLM
  • Two built: University of Raffel Luo and Pathum Rai District
  • Tested, not complete yet: we are measuring defences against tricky prompts

Banner shows

Questions glide along the band into a shield; past it, each one is "answered from its documents". Stamped IN PROGRESS. (SAMPLE.)

Points to cover

  • RAG = retrieval-augmented generation: first search the assistant's own documents for the passages that answer the question, then a small language model writes the reply from them.
  • Like the other models, it runs fully in the browser (WebLLM, Qwen2.5-1.5B for answers, snowflake-arctic-embed-s for search).
  • Two assistants are built: a university helpdesk (University of Raffel Luo) and a district emergency helpdesk (Pathum Rai District).
  • Status: tested, not complete. We are testing how well defences hold up against tricky prompts, one defence at a time and then all together. Don't quote results yet; they aren't final.
